# Cook’s membrane at finite strain

This is an original command recipe for the plane-strain panel in C. D. Foster and
T. Mohammad Nejad, *Trilinear Hexahedra with Integral-Averaged Volumes for Nearly
Incompressible Nonlinear Deformation*, Engineering 7:765–788 (2015),
[DOI 10.4236/eng.2015.711067](https://doi.org/10.4236/eng.2015.711067),
[authors’ published paper](https://content.scirp.org/pdf/eng_2015113016463684.pdf).
Only the authors’ published numbers and mathematical definition are used; no input
files, figures, code or commercial-code comparison numbers are reproduced.

## Transcription and model

Equations (57)–(59), p. 773, use the same decoupled neo-Hookean energy as FieldCraft:

`W = μ/2 (J^(-2/3) tr(C) - 3) + K/2 (J - 1)^2`.

Section 5.2, p. 775, specifies μ = 0.8 and K = 4000 N/mm² and a total upward force
of 1 N. Figure 8, p. 778, defines the corners (0,0), (48,44), (48,60), (0,44) mm,
1 mm thickness, a clamped left edge and all out-of-plane motion held. The right
edge is 16 mm long: the uniform dead traction is 1/16 N/mm² over its 16 mm² face,
not 1 N per node or per unit area. The command uses `distribution: "total"`.
The top-right corner C is (48,60,0) mm.

Table 4, p. 778, gives **6.0320, 6.6318, 6.8279 mm** for 16, 64, 256 elements in
the authors’ **J averaged in reference configuration** column. The separate ANSYS
column is excluded. Figure 9, p. 779, shows the regular mapped 16×16 mesh.
The recipe uses 4×4×1, 8×8×1 and 16×16×1 trilinear hybrid hex8 meshes; each is a
separate model. Left-face fixing and z-symmetry on both thickness faces enforce
plane strain. FieldCraft’s constant-pressure hybrid hex8 uses the reference-volume
mean J. This is the corresponding mean-dilatation comparison, not the paper’s
current-volume averaging variant or an enhanced-strain element.

The display preset is `mm-t-s-N-MPa`; internal storage remains SI. The original
material inputs follow `E = 9Kμ/(3K+μ) = 2.399840010665956 MPa` and
`ν = (3K-2μ)/(2(3K+μ)) = 0.49990000666622225`. No alternate neo-Hookean energy
with an unmodified first invariant or logarithmic volumetric term is substituted.

## Prescribed-mesh comparison and refinement

The following independent meshes use the same mapping, thickness, material, load
and solver settings. Displacements are in mm; the published column is transcribed,
and the FieldCraft column is generated by this recipe (with divisions changed for
32 and 64). A dash means no reference was transcribed for that mesh.

| Divisions | Hex8 elements | Nodes | FieldCraft Uy(C) | Published Uy(C) | Relative difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 4×4×1 | 16 | 50 | 6.032603352817065 | 6.0320 | 0.010003% |
| 8×8×1 | 64 | 162 | 6.632338934849449 | 6.6318 | 0.008127% |
| 16×16×1 | 256 | 578 | 6.828483038210331 | 6.8279 | 0.008539% |
| 32×32×1 | 1024 | 2178 | 6.8983555772676155 | — | — |
| 64×64×1 | 4096 | 8450 | 6.925643764325844 | — | — |

The 16→32 and 32→64 changes are about 1.02% and 0.40%. Thus 6.8279 is a
**finite-mesh reference**, not a converged continuum answer. Neither the 32/64
results nor a finer hex20 solution are checked against it. The oracle follows the
benchmark-prescribed-mesh option in `docs/verification.md`.

The reference tolerance is the **0.5% independent-implementation comparison policy
already proposed in issue #83**, retained without fitting to these results. It is
**not** a source uncertainty, a discretization-error bound or a consequence of the
printed precision. Four decimal places imply rounding of at most ±0.00005 mm;
our differences (about 0.00054–0.00060 mm) exceed that rounding. The paper does not
report enough numerical settings to establish the cause. This case demonstrates
agreement within the declared policy, not reproduction to every printed digit.
The fixture separately guards FieldCraft’s accepted result against later drift.

## Bounded reproduction

```
cargo xtask oracle --case nl-cook-membrane
cargo run --release -p fieldcraft-cli -- run tests/oracle/nl-cook-membrane/recipe.json
```

The ordinary oracle runs only 4, 8 and 16 divisions, leaving the solved 16×16 model
available for inspection. For a refinement run, take one complete model block
(from `file.new` through `solve.probe`) and change both in-plane divisions to 32
or 64. Keep its thickness division at 1. Do not bless either as a new reference.

Each solve caps 100,000 dofs, 20,000,000 factor entries, 1,000 total Newton
iterations and three worker threads. The 64×64 study used 25,350 dofs and
2,191,872 factor entries. Initial and maximum increments are 0.1, minimum 0.00001,
maximum 100 increments and 20 iterations per increment; automatic cutbacks are
allowed. Increment/load factor is quasistatic continuation, not physical time.

As a solver sensitivity control on the 16×16 mesh, changing the default convergence
thresholds (force 1e-6, displacement 1e-5, energy 1e-10) to (1e-9, 1e-8, 1e-12)
gave Uy = 6.828483038210330 mm. Keeping those tighter thresholds and limiting
increments to 0.05 gave 6.828483038210328 mm. This checks our iterative sensitivity
only; it does not bound the paper’s unknown numerical error or continuum error.
No mechanics, constitutive law or solver tolerance default is changed by this case.
